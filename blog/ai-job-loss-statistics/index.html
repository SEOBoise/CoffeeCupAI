01 The Headline Numbers

AI used to be a footnote in layoff announcements. In 2026 it became the reason companies give most often. The trackers below count what employers say when they cut jobs, and that count has risen faster than anything else in the labor data.

121,401
US job cuts where employers cited AI, January–October 2026[1]
2.2×

40%
Share of all May 2026 US layoffs attributed to AI, a monthly record[2]
128,536
Tech workers laid off worldwide by Sept 10, 2026, already more than all of 2025[3]
  • Outplacement firm Challenger, Gray & Christmas has tracked AI as a layoff reason since 2023. Employers cited it for 12,742 cuts in 2024 and 54,836 in 2025. By the end of May 2026 the count had reached 87,714, more than the previous two years combined.[4]
  • AI's share of announced US cuts rose from 7% in January 2026 to 26% in April and 40% in May. May's 38,579 AI-attributed cuts were the most Challenger has recorded in a single month.[2]
  • By May, AI was the most-cited reason for layoffs for the third month running. Through the first five months of 2026 it accounted for 22% of all announced US job cuts.[2]
  • In April 2026, Andy Challenger said that whether or not AI is doing a given person's job, the money for that role is being redirected to it.[5]
  • Technology accounts for 31% of all US job cuts announced in 2026, and AI remains the reason tech companies give most often.[6]
  • US tech employers announced 123,653 cuts in the first five months of 2026, up 66% from the same period in 2025.[7] By mid-year the sector had announced 139,156 cuts, up 83% year over year, while total US cuts across all sectors fell 40%.[8]
  • Fintech firms announced 5,731 cuts in May 2026, and most of them were attributed to AI.[9]
  • Layoffs.fyi counted 128,536 tech workers laid off at 299 companies from January 1 to September 10, 2026, already more than the 122,606 it recorded for all of 2025.[3]
  • Layoffs.fyi founder Roger Lee says AI was cited in 33% of tech layoff events in 2026, up from 1% in 2024. His tracker attributes 92,913 layoffs, 72% of this year's total, to AI.[10]

02 What the Big Forecasts Say

Major banks, international institutions and research labs have tried to measure how much of today's work AI can already do, or soon will.

300M
Jobs worldwide exposed to AI automation (Goldman Sachs)[11]
60%
Jobs in advanced economies exposed to AI (IMF)[12]
92M
Jobs projected to be displaced globally by 2030 (WEF)[13]
11.7%
Share of the US workforce, about $1.2T in wages, that current AI can already replace (MIT)[14]
  • Goldman Sachs estimates that about 300 million jobs worldwide are exposed to AI automation, that AI could automate tasks making up 25% of all US work hours, and that 6–7% of workers could be displaced over a roughly ten-year adoption period.[11] Its earlier task analysis put office and administrative support exposure at 46% and legal at 44%.[15]
  • The International Monetary Fund found about 40% of jobs worldwide, and 60% in advanced economies, exposed to AI. For about half of the exposed jobs, the IMF says AI could take over key tasks, lowering demand for labor and wages, and in the most extreme cases some jobs could disappear.[12]
  • In October 2026, IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva called AI's effect on employment a slow-moving tsunami. She warned that disappearing routine jobs could remove the traditional entry routes into professional careers.[16]
  • The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 projects that 92 million jobs will be displaced by 2030 and that 22% of all jobs will be disrupted. It found that 41% of employers plan to reduce their workforce as AI automates tasks.[13] It also estimates that more than 120 million workers face medium-term risk of redundancy because they are unlikely to get the reskilling they need.[17]
  • MIT and Oak Ridge National Laboratory's Iceberg Index simulates 151 million US workers. It finds that current AI can already replace 11.7% of the labor market, about $1.2 trillion in wages. Only 2.2% (about $211 billion) is visible in today's adoption patterns; the rest sits below the waterline in administrative, financial and professional services.[14] The tool covers more than 32,000 skills across 923 occupations.[18]
  • Bloomberg Intelligence surveyed technology chiefs at 93 major banks and projects that global banks could cut as many as 200,000 jobs within three to five years as AI takes over tasks.[19] On average the executives expect to cut about 3% of their workforce.[20]
  • Citi has estimated that more than half (54%) of banking jobs are at high risk of automation.[21]

03 What the CEOs Are Saying

Some of the starkest predictions come from the executives building or deploying the technology.

  • Mark Zuckerberg said he expects 2026 to be the year AI dramatically changes how Meta works.[29]
  • Dorsey predicted that within a year most companies would make similar structural cuts. He said he chose to make one deep cut now instead of trimming gradually.[27]
  • Wells Fargo CEO Charlie Scharf said anyone claiming AI won't reduce headcount either doesn't understand it or isn't being honest.[30]
  • Ford's Farley said tech companies' hiring of entry-level workers has dropped 50% since 2019 and warned that up to half of US white-collar jobs could disappear within a decade.[24] JPMorgan's consumer banking chief expects operations headcount to fall about 10% as the bank adopts AI.[31]
  • IBM CEO Arvind Krishna said 30% of the company's roughly 26,000 non-customer-facing roles could be replaced by AI and automation within five years, about 7,800 jobs, and that hiring for those roles would pause.[32, 33]

Case files: the clearest examples

Salesforce: 9,000 → 5,000

In September 2025, Marc Benioff said Salesforce had cut its customer support workforce from about 9,000 to about 5,000. AI agents now handle around half of all customer conversations, and AI has handled more than a million of them.[65] AI is also working through about 10,000 sales leads a week.[26] ServiceNow, a peer, expected to save $100 million in staffing costs from its own internal use of AI.[79]

Block: cutting nearly half while growing

Block was not in trouble when it cut more than 4,000 people; Dorsey said profits and customer numbers were still growing.[27] The company expects $450–500 million in restructuring charges as it moves to what it calls an AI-embedded operating model.[46]

Klarna: 700 agents' worth of work

A month after launch, Klarna's OpenAI-powered assistant had held 2.3 million conversations, two-thirds of all customer service chats, and cut average resolution time from 11 minutes to 2.[78] Klarna stopped most hiring and let attrition shrink headcount 22% to 3,500 in a year.[81] In May 2025 it began recruiting human agents again after quality slipped.[82]

Dow: AI comes to heavy industry

Chemicals giant Dow is cutting about 12.5% of its workforce and naming AI automation as the driver.[48] Dow previously sought $1 billion in cost savings and announced about 1,500 cuts in January 2025.[47]

05 Cutting Payroll to Pay for AI Infrastructure

The second wave isn't about AI doing a specific person's job. It's about companies moving money out of salaries and into data centers, chips and power.

$125–145B

60,000

  • Bloomberg describes the roughly $650 billion that Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft plan to spend in 2026 as a boom without parallel this century.[83] The four companies' 2026 budgets exceed the combined spending of 21 of the largest US industrial companies.[84]
  • In 2025, Meta spent more on capital projects than on research and development (mostly engineers' salaries) for the first time in six years.[84]
  • Alphabet forecast up to $185 billion in 2026 capex, up 102%. Amazon announced $200 billion, up 56%.[85] Amazon later raised its 2026 forecast to $220 billion.[86]
  • Amazon's capital spending reached $131.8 billion in 2025, up from $83 billion the year before, as layoffs continued.[87]
  • Microsoft's 2025 cuts of more than 15,000 jobs, about 7% of its workforce, came during record profits. Analysts described them as a realignment to fund an $80 billion push into AI infrastructure.[72]
  • Analyst Gil Luria told Reuters that Microsoft might keep laying off about 10,000 workers a year to offset the higher depreciation from its capital spending.[88]
  • Meta's 2026 capex guidance of $115–135 billion is nearly double the $72.2 billion it spent in 2025, itself a record.[89] After it raised the plan again and announced layoffs, Meta's stock fell about 10%.[90]
  • Bloomberg first reported in March 2026 that Oracle planned to cut thousands of jobs, in part because of its huge data-center spending.[91] TD Cowen estimated cuts of 20,000–30,000 could free $8–10 billion a year in cash flow for data-center construction.[92]
  • Oracle began another round of layoffs on September 14, 2026, and raised its restructuring budget to $2.8 billion.[93]
  • By earlier in 2026, Layoffs.fyi had already counted more than 119,800 tech layoffs at nearly 200 companies, even as AI infrastructure budgets hit records.[94]

06 Buyouts, Voluntary Exits and Quiet Shrinking

Not every reduction is a layoff. Companies are increasingly offering severance to people who leave voluntarily, often framed around "the pace" of AI change.

  • On October 26, 2026, Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, roughly 27% of its global workforce, as it spends heavily on AI. The AP reported that data-center investment would likely top $700 billion this year.[34]
  • Microsoft's offer covers employees at level 70 and below in the US, UK, Canada and India, and follows a voluntary retirement program earlier in 2026.[35]
  • Microsoft and Google are both offering voluntary exits while continuing heavy AI investment.[95] In the same quarter it announced the buyouts, Microsoft reported $45.2 billion in capital spending.[96]
  • Google has run voluntary exit programs since 2025. In June 2026 it expanded them to Canada, France, Germany, Japan and Switzerland, after earlier offers in the US and UK.[36]
  • In February 2026, Google offered voluntary exits to staff in its Global Business Organization who were not ready to go "all in" on AI. It was the company's third buyout program in eight months, and YouTube also offered buyouts during its October 2025 AI-focused reorganization.[97, 98]
  • Google's 2025 buyouts covered US employees in search, ads, research and engineering. They followed a January 2025 program in its Platforms & Devices division (Android, Chrome and Pixel).[99]
  • Klarna shrank largely by not replacing departing employees, using AI to absorb the work.[81] IBM said part of any reduction would come from not refilling roles vacated by attrition.[32]
  • JPMorgan's CFO asked managers to resist headcount growth where possible.[31] In Q3 2025 its profit rose 12% to $14.4 billion while headcount grew 1%.[100]

07 Banking and Finance

  • The six largest US banks shed 15,000 jobs in the first quarter of 2026 while posting $47 billion in profits. Citigroup's headcount fell by 2,000 in a single quarter, and Wells Fargo's is down 65,000 since 2019.[30]
  • JPMorgan expects to cut operations staff, including fraud, statements and account services, by 10%.[31]
  • Goldman Sachs launched "OneGS 3.0," an AI-driven reorganization. CEO David Solomon said the bank would constrain headcount growth and make limited cuts, even after quarterly profit jumped 37% to $4.1 billion.[100]
  • In October 2025, Goldman told employees to expect possible job cuts and slower hiring as it used AI to raise productivity. PNC's CEO said the bank's headcount equals what it was 10 years ago, when the bank was a third of its current size.[101]
  • Bloomberg Intelligence projects up to 200,000 global banking job cuts in three to five years.[19] About a quarter of bank technology chiefs predicted a 5–10% headcount decline.[21]

08 Young Workers and the Vanishing First Rung

The clearest early signal is at the bottom of the career ladder, where AI substitutes most directly for junior work.

−32%

43%

  • Stanford's "Canaries in the Coal Mine" study used millions of ADP payroll records. It found that employment for 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs, such as software development and customer service, fell 13% relative to less-exposed roles, while older workers in the same occupations held steady or grew.[106]
  • The August 2026 revision widened the gap to 19% below where it would have been. It found the declines concentrated where AI substitutes for human tasks, and that the adjustment is happening through fewer jobs rather than lower pay.[102]
  • Stanford and ADP's Canaries Dashboard shows employment for 22–25-year-olds in highly exposed roles shrinking 3.8% a year as of April 2026, while the same age group grew 2% a year in the least-exposed roles.[107]
  • The New York Fed puts unemployment for recent college graduates at 5.7% in Q4 2025, a four-year high. Nearly 9 in 10 graduates worry AI will replace entry-level roles.[108] By March 2026 the rate was 5.6%, against 4.3% nationally.[109]
  • Recent-grad unemployment has exceeded the national rate for five straight years, which was almost unheard of between 1990 and 2018. Computer engineering (7.8%) and computer science (7.0%) graduates had some of the highest rates, and a February 2026 Dallas Fed paper found AI reducing entry-level hiring in exposed occupations.[110]
  • Indeed's chief economist says more tech employers are moving away from entry-level hiring, with more postings asking for three to five years of experience.[111]
  • In the UK, Adzuna found entry-level vacancies (graduate jobs, apprenticeships, internships and junior roles) down 32% since November 2022. They now make up 25% of postings, down from nearly 29%.[104] Entry-level IT vacancies fell 54.8% and entry-level accounting and finance 50.8%.[112]
  • Mercer's Global Talent Trends 2026 found the share of companies reducing junior positions jumped from 17% to 43% in one year, and flagged 22–27-year-olds as facing heightened displacement risk.[105]

09 Freelancers and Creative Work

  • An analysis of 5 million Upwork postings from November 2022 to February 2024 found writing jobs down 33%, translation down 19% and customer service down 16%.[113]
  • Researchers at Washington University and NYU found that after ChatGPT's release, freelance writers on Upwork saw monthly jobs fall 2% and monthly earnings fall 5.2%.[114]
  • A Complexity Science Hub study found demand for easily replaced tasks fell sharply. Jobs writing "About Us" pages dropped 50% and Western-language translation work 30%, with experienced workers in those skills losing the most demand.[115]
  • Duolingo had already cut about 10% of its contractors after switching to AI translation before announcing its "AI-first" plan.[75]

10 "Prove AI Can't Do It": The New Hiring Rule

Some of the most consequential changes don't show up as layoffs at all. They're jobs that never get posted.

  • Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke told teams to show why they “cannot get what they want done using AI” before asking for more headcount.[116] Questions about AI use were added to performance and peer reviews.[117]
  • Duolingo said new positions now require a justification that the work cannot be automated. Its CEO accepted a possible small dip in quality to move faster.[75]
  • IBM said it would suspend or slow hiring in back-office functions such as HR.[33]
  • Klarna stopped hiring and let attrition do the work.[81]
  • Meta cancelled plans to fill 6,000 open positions alongside its 8,000 layoffs.[41]

  • An AIResumeBuilder survey of 1,250 leaders found 3 in 10 companies plan to replace employees with AI in 2026. Among companies expecting AI-related layoffs, 59% plan to eliminate at least 10% of their workforce. Customer support (54%), administrative (49%) and IT support (47%) roles are most at risk.[123]

  • A ResumeBuilder survey found 54% of companies have cut or will cut employee pay to free up capital for AI spending in 2026.[124]
  • In Pew's June 2026 survey, 71% of US adults expect AI to mean fewer jobs over the next 20 years, up from 64% in 2024. Among adults under 30 the share rose from 61% to 73%.[121, 125]

  • Pew's survey of workers found 52% worried about AI's impact on their jobs, and only 6% thought it would bring them more job opportunities.[127]

13 Reading These Numbers Carefully

The figures above are real, but how much of the job loss AI actually causes is still disputed. Readers deserve the full picture:

  • Challenger counts the reasons employers state. By October 2026, AI was cited for only 1,265 cuts, under 3% of the month and the lowest monthly count of the year.[1] Hiring was also up 25% from 2025, and Challenger said AI-related cutting has been limited outside tech.[6]
  • A Yale Budget Lab analysis in October 2025 found no discernible disruption to the broader US labor market in the 33 months after ChatGPT's release.[129] A September 2026 IZA study found recent-graduate unemployment in summer 2026 was not unusually high compared with earlier years.[130]
  • OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has said some companies are "AI washing," blaming AI for layoffs they would have made anyway, while acknowledging real displacement too.[131] Forrester has warned that many companies announcing AI layoffs don't yet have mature AI ready to fill those roles.[132]
  • Amazon's Andy Jassy said the October 2025 cuts were about culture, not money or AI, "not right now."[133] TCS's CEO likewise denied AI drove its cuts.[69]
  • Some replacements have reversed. Klarna resumed hiring human agents after quality fell[82], and Commonwealth Bank reinstated 45 roles it had handed to a voice bot.[67]
  • Layoffs.fyi's Roger Lee notes there has been little evidence that AI is actually doing the work of the people let go in the largest AI-attributed layoffs.[10]

Either way, the money is moving. Whether AI is doing the work yet or not, companies are cutting payroll, freezing hiring and offering buyouts in its name, and redirecting hundreds of billions of dollars toward it.
