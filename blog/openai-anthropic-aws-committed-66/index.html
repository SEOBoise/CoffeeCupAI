The deployment economy

OpenAI, Anthropic, and AWS committed $6.6 billion to deploying AI inside companies. Six months in, the evidence is mostly speed.

In the six months since the frontier labs launched their deployment arms, a market that did not exist in 2025 has raised capital, hired engineers, and landed its first clients.

By Dan ODonnell  ·  Coffee Cup AI  ·  November 25, 2026

Within 72 hours this May, OpenAI and Anthropic each launched a private-equity-backed company whose product is people: engineers embedded inside customer organizations to make AI work. Amazon Web Services committed a billion dollars to its own version weeks later. By mid-October, the combined commitments totaled at least $6.6 billion, all aimed at the same diagnosis. The bottleneck is getting the models into daily operations, a shift that makes AI for business less about model access and more about implementation. That is also where AI consulting has to prove its value: connecting workflows to measurable operating outcomes.

Six months in, the first measurable outputs of that money are landing, and they arrive in a very specific order of evidence: adoption (logos and seat counts), productivity (tasks done faster), and financial returns (dollars returned to the business). The frontier labs and their partners have produced plenty of the first two and, by their own admission, almost none of the third. That evidence gap echoes our review of the 95% AI pilot failure claim, where a dramatic headline obscured the harder question of measurable return.

That gap is the story of this market right now, because the labs did not spend $6.6 billion to earn services fees. They spent it to demonstrate the returns that keep the rest of the AI economy financed.

01 · The tally: four vehicles, $6.6 billion

The money has gone into four distinct vehicles, each structured differently. OpenAI launched the Deployment Company, known internally as DeployCo, with more than $4 billion from 19 investment firms, consultancies, and system integrators, led by TPG with Advent, Bain Capital, and Brookfield as co-leads. Consulting investors include McKinsey, Bain & Company, and Capgemini. The company is majority-owned by OpenAI, and it bought the applied-AI firm Tomoro to acquire roughly 150 forward-deployed engineers on day one. Anthropic and Blackstone built a $1.5 billion implementation firm now called Ode, seeded with the acquired boutique Fractional AI and currently employing about 100 engineers. AWS is spending $1 billion on an internal forward-deployed engineering organization. And Anthropic is putting $100 million into its Claude Frontier Academy to train 10,000 engineers by the end of 2027.

All four vehicles orbit one job title: the forward-deployed engineer, or FDE, a role Palantir popularized two decades ago and the AI economy has repriced into something scarce. FDEs combine software skill with enough understanding of how a company runs to get a model wired into real workflows, security rules, and organizational politics. OpenAI describes a typical DeployCo engagement as a diagnostic, a short list of priority workflows, and then engineers working inside the customer's organization to build, test, and deploy production systems.

02 · Why the money moved in May

The labs launched their deployment arms in the same season that Wall Street started asking what all the AI spending was returning. The week CNBC reported the FDE hiring surge, AI-themed stocks from Dell to CoreWeave, AMD, and Micron sold off sharply. "The Street is starting to become impatient with these billions of companies who are spending on AI without getting any business outcomes," Jeff Christian, founder of executive search firm Christian & Timbers, told CNBC.

Every dollar a customer spends on compute, seats, or API calls now demands a demonstrated return, and the labs discovered that the return is gated by something they do not sell: the messy work of integration. PwC partner Sanjay Subramanian, who leads the firm's Anthropic alliance, put the mechanism plainly: "The quality of these models is going up and up. The ability for companies to deploy them is not keeping up. That gap is increasing."

There is also a structural commercial logic, and it favors the labs over every independent consultancy. Deployment work itself is a small slice of what an AI system costs over its life. An analysis by research firm Plank puts deployment at roughly 20 percent of lifetime cost; the other 80 percent arrives later, as maintenance, iteration, and scaling, typically paid to whoever built the system and on whose stack. A lab that deploys its own engineers into your workflows is after more than a services fee: it wants the relationship that determines which models your company runs for years. The venture capitalist Chamath Palihapitiya saw the same move from the consultants' side and called it what it looked like: "you are letting the fox into the henhouse."

03 · The labor arms race

The capital commitments are promises; the hiring data shows what companies are doing. U.S. job postings for forward-deployed engineers went from fewer than 200 in all of 2023 to roughly 1,200 in 2025 to more than 7,300 in the first nine months of 2026, according to labor-market analytics firm Lightcast. Median advertised pay reached $195,456, up 40 percent from 2023 and more than triple the median across all U.S. postings.

The biggest recruiters of forward-deployed engineers are the consulting firms the labs are coming for.

The supply math explains the academy. Lightcast's head of global research, Elena Magrini, notes the FDE role shares roughly half its skills with software engineering, which is the only pool big enough to feed it. Anthropic's Claude Frontier Academy borrows the structure of medical training: residency cohorts, realistic enterprise cases, assessment before independent work, then 12 weeks of mentorship from Anthropic engineers. Its first cohorts are staff from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, and Novo Nordisk.

A live census by research firm Plank counted 1,143 open FDE postings across 462 companies as of mid-November, with a median posted base salary of $202,000. The count comes with caveats. A posting is not a hire, one requisition can cover many seats, and at the frontier labs equity is the majority of compensation. Even at that, the census confirms the shape of the market: demand is concentrated at the top and spreading fast through the long tail, where growth-stage companies hire their first FDE as a signal they are moving upmarket.

04 · The receipts, audited

Six months of operation have produced a stack of case studies, and they are almost all denominated in speed. Sort the evidence into three tiers and you can see how much of it is self-reported. The same distinction between arithmetic and proof shapes our audit of Replit's 150x productivity claim.

The market is young and the vendors are grading their own work, which is a different claim than the one their capital commitments imply. A McKinsey survey released alongside its November 17 alliance with Cognition found the same split inside customer organizations: the top quarter of companies report productivity gains above 2x from AI in software development, while 30 percent report productivity that went down. McKinsey traces the difference to whether companies redesigned how the work gets done, which is the job the human layer is selling. So far, the engineering gains are documented and the financial returns are not.

"The AI skills gap affects every customer and every partner, so it is a market problem."STEVE CORFIELD, ANTHROPIC, ON WHY THE LABS ARE TRAINING THE WORKFORCE THEMSELVES

05 · Who gets served, and who gets skipped

The new market's tier structure was designed in. The WSJ's CIO Journal reported DeployCo would give its first engagements to large companies with longstanding OpenAI relationships. The New Stack placed Ode one segment below, in mid-sized enterprises the big consultancies do not prioritize, and named community banks, regional health systems, and mid-market manufacturers. AWS serves its broad enterprise base. That ordering leaves the smallest buyers with a specific, smaller set of options.

At a median advertised salary near $195,000, a single embedded engineer costs a quarter million dollars a year before overhead, and these firms sell full teams. An engagement model built around embedded squads priced for private-equity returns points upmarket by construction. This is our reading of the structure (none of the firms have said so), but the arithmetic is simple: the lab layer will not reach down into small business, and nothing announced in the last six months suggests it intends to.

For mid-market and small-business buyers, the middle of the market is thinning. The labs take the top, the Big Four scale FDE practices for the tier below, and independents compete for everyone else with one durable advantage: model neutrality. DeployCo builds OpenAI-first, Ode operates "Claude-first," and AWS works AWS-aligned, each for sensible commercial reasons. A buyer whose real asset is the flexibility to use whichever model fits the workflow will pay a premium for that neutrality, and should know it is paying for it.

06 · The incumbents hedge four ways

The established firms' own moves show how seriously they take the threat. McKinsey, Bain & Company, and Capgemini are investors in DeployCo, the company built to serve their best clients. Deloitte, Accenture, McKinsey, and Bain send their own engineers into Anthropic's academy. Deloitte and Accenture simultaneously run the largest FDE recruiting operations in the country, building rival practices. And McKinsey, this week, announced a strategic alliance with Cognition to deploy its Devin software agent across client engineering organizations.

Palihapitiya's warning was aimed at exactly this: consultancies that deploy the labs' products are feeding usage data and deployment patterns back to companies that now own competing services arms. Caylent's Jason Cutler, whose firm runs a dedicated Anthropic practice, disagrees and reads the labs' entry as validation that the work exists. Both readings can hold for the next 12 months, and the margin numbers will decide between them after that.

07 · If you are buying AI help in 2027

Ask what the receipt is denominated in, before you sign anything. If a vendor's evidence is speed and seat counts, ask for the financial baseline it is supposed to move and how the gain will be measured. The labs themselves plan to track time-to-production and usage breadth; those are vendor metrics. Revenue per workflow hour, cost per processed transaction, and error rates are buyer metrics. Insist on the second kind, with a baseline captured before work begins.

Price the lock-in you are accepting. A lab's deployment arm will do excellent work on the lab's stack, and deployment is only about 20 percent of what the system costs over its life, by Plank's estimate. The model choice made during those first engagements compounds into the other 80 percent. If neutrality matters to you, say so in the scope and expect to pay for it.

Match the seller to your size. The $6.6 billion layer is engineered for enterprises and, through Ode, down into the mid-market. Below that, your realistic field is independents, cloud partner programs, and eventually the certified graduates of programs like the Frontier Academy. The firms serving this tier are the only ones in the market whose incentives are aligned with your workflow rather than with a model vendor's share of it. Federal Reserve research on small-business AI adoption shows why that implementation gap matters: most firms are still early, even when adopters are more likely to hire.

Mark the calendar. The academy's first credentials arrive early in 2027, DeployCo's first cohort of engagements will be up for renewal within the year, and the labs have staked their investor relationships on producing business outcomes. If the receipts do not move from speed to dollars over the next two quarters, the Street's impatience will reach the human layer too. Either way, the buyers who demanded financial metrics from day one will have the numbers to show for it.
