The focus

Turn AI curiosity
into conversation.

Give your audience a clear, engaging way to think about artificial intelligence—and a reason to keep the conversation going.

An AI keynote speaker should help people connect the technology to decisions they actually face. Which tasks could benefit from automation? Where does human judgment matter most? What would a useful, responsible first experiment look like?

Dan brings an agency operator’s perspective to those questions. His work connects AI consulting, SEO, digital marketing, and employee development. The emphasis is on understanding the work, testing an idea, and deciding what deserves to change.

Keynotes introduce a shared way to think about AI. Workshops give people room to apply it. Leadership briefings make space for the decisions behind adoption.

Speaking topics

Ideas your audience
can take with them.

Choose a starting point. The examples, level of detail, and discussion can be shaped around your audience and the decisions ahead of them.

01 / UNDERSTAND✳

AI in plain English

From unfamiliar technology to better questions.

An accessible conversation about what AI can do, what it can get wrong, and how to ask better questions. Explore the differences between machine learning, generative AI, and workflow automation through recognizable business examples.

Discuss how large language models produce responses, why a confident answer can still be wrong, and how to evaluate an output before relying on it. Examples can include drafting a customer response or turning meeting notes into a plan.

Audience takeaway

A way to judge a proposed AI use case: what goes in, what comes out, and who checks the result.

For: business conferences, associations, and teams starting with AI.

02 / APPLY↗

AI and the everyday workplace

Find the useful work. Keep people accountable.

Explore practical applications, the role of human judgment, and how teams can approach change together. Start with a workflow, then consider the data, tools, review steps, and training it needs.

Sessions can explore AI adoption in marketing, customer service, research, or internal operations. Discuss how to compare the time saved with the time spent checking results—and how quality, privacy, and rework affect the value of a pilot.

Audience takeaway

A practical pilot outline with a clear task, an accountable person, and a meaningful measure of success.

For: executives, business owners, operations leaders, and managers.

03 / ADAPT◎

The human side of the shift

Skills, uncertainty, and the future of work.

Create space to talk about skills, uncertainty, opportunity, and the concerns people bring to AI. What changes when a tool can draft, summarize, or analyze? What still depends on experience, relationships, and good judgment?

Explore how employees can examine the tasks in their role, practice with appropriate information, and strengthen their ability to question and improve AI-assisted work. Leaders can consider how to support learning and explain changes clearly.

Audience takeaway

A starting point for a personal learning plan and a more useful conversation about changing work.

For: employee events, HR and learning teams, and leadership groups.

Consider a customer-service team using AI to draft replies. The interesting questions go beyond whether the draft sounds convincing.

  1. 01

    Choose the task

  2. 02

For event organizers

Built around
your audience.

A useful AI keynote starts with the people in the room. Share their roles, familiarity with AI, and the question you want the session to help answer.

01 / BEFORE THE EVENT

Give the talk a purpose

Identify the event theme and one or two desired takeaways. A leadership group deciding where to invest needs a different conversation from employees trying AI for the first time.

Discuss your industry, vocabulary, constraints, and examples that will feel familiar. Customization can shape both the presentation and audience Q&A.

02 / IN THE ROOM

Make participation practical

Agree on the balance of presentation, questions, discussion, and exercises. For any live demonstration, discuss account access, Wi-Fi, accessibility, and a fallback if a tool is unavailable.

Use approved, public, or fictional information for examples. Make the role of human review visible as part of the demonstration.

03 / AFTER THE SESSION

Give the ideas somewhere to go

Decide how your organization will follow up. Ask participants to name one task worth exploring, the person responsible for it, and the evidence they would need before expanding a pilot.

A short follow-up discussion can help you learn what the audience understood and what support they need next.

Booking questions

A few good
questions.

The details that help you plan a useful session.

What kinds of sessions can we discuss?

Keynotes, interactive workshops, leadership briefings, and facilitated discussions. Share the format you have in mind, your preferred session length, and whether the event is in person or virtual. Availability and delivery details are confirmed when your brief is discussed.

Can the content fit our audience?

Yes. Tell us who is attending, how familiar they are with AI, and what you want them to take away. The level of detail, business examples, and audience discussion can be shaped around your group. Nontechnical audiences can start with AI in plain English; experienced teams can focus on implementation decisions.

What does it cost to book Dan as an AI speaker?

Request a quote for your event. The fee and scope depend on the format, preparation, session length, location or virtual delivery, and any agreed travel or recording arrangements. Include your budget range in the inquiry so the conversation starts with a practical brief.

Can we include live demonstrations or hands-on exercises?

These can be discussed as part of the session plan. Confirm the tools, account access, technical setup, and information participants may use. Public or fictional examples can support the exercise, with a fallback plan if a live tool or connection is unavailable.

What should an event inquiry include?

Include your event date, location or virtual format, audience, session length, and the topic you would like to explore. Share your budget range, desired takeaways, accessibility needs, and any recording or distribution requirements. Those details help establish fit, scope, and availability.
